Why check

From abroad, you cannot see what the seller is not telling you.

Sold twice

The same plot sold to more than one buyer, each with papers that look real.

No right to sell

A seller who is not the owner, or a family member selling without the family head's consent.

Land you can't build on

A plot inside a government acquisition, a road reserve, a wetland or a flood zone.

Trouble on site

Someone already living or farming on the plot, or people claiming it by force.

What we check

Seven checks, one signed report.

Every report follows the same template. Each check says what we found, shows the evidence and gets its own verdict.

  1. 01

    Plot identity

    The plot you are being sold is the one on the site plan, and the one the surveyor finds on the ground.

  2. 02

    Lands Commission searches

    Official searches on the plot and on the seller's name: registered interests, caveats and pending applications.

  3. 03

    Surveyor's check

    A licensed surveyor visits, picks up the pillars and compares the ground to the site plan and the official records.

  4. 04

    Map checks

    Government acquisitions, road reserves, wetlands, flood zones, known dispute areas and zoning.

  5. 05

    Seller's right to sell

    Who is selling and who must consent. The stool, skin or family head confirms, on film, with witnesses.

  6. 06

    Chain of ownership

    Earlier transfers, indentures, receipts and any allocation note from the traditional authority.

  7. 07

    Who is on the land

    Anyone living, farming or building on it now, and any sign of an active dispute.

The verdict

A clear answer, with what to do next.

A report cannot be Green if any single check is Red.

Green

No material issue found within scope. You may proceed, subject to the conditions listed.

Amber

Issues that can be fixed before you buy. The report says what must happen and who must do it. Do not pay until they are fixed.

Red

A serious problem you cannot fix, or a risk we will not advise you to take. Do not buy.

How it works

Four steps, and you see each one.

  1. Tell us about the plot

    Where it is, who is selling, and any papers you have, such as a site plan or an indenture.

  2. Agree the price

    We quote before we start. You pay Tenmina's fee only once you accept the quote.

  3. We check

    Searches, a site visit and the seller's consent on film. We update you on WhatsApp or email as each step is done.

  4. Get your report

    A signed report with photos, maps and the evidence for every check, and a fingerprint so you can tell it has not been changed.

Who does the work

Licensed people, on the ground in Ghana.

  • A lawyerruns the searches, reviews the papers, attends the consent meeting and signs the legal sections.
  • A licensed surveyorvisits the plot, finds the pillars and signs the surveyor's section.
  • A field runnerlodges the searches, chases results and takes the photos and video.

Two people review every report before you get it.

What the report is not

We say plainly what we cannot promise.

  • It does not guarantee title. Only registration at the Lands Commission confirms title, and registered title can be challenged.
  • It does not replace legal advice on your purchase agreement.
  • It relies on official records and on what the seller and witnesses tell us, which we record but cannot prove.
  • It describes the position on the dates of the searches and the site visit, for the plot named in the report only.

Every report includes the full scope statement.
